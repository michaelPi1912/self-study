## Case P35: One bad message, and everything behind it is stuck

**Interview prompt.** Hi, here is an on-call story from our messaging team. We have a Spring Boot consumer that reads order events from a Kafka topic. This morning the dashboard showed that the consumer lag on one partition was growing fast, while the other partitions looked normal. In the logs we saw the same error again and again: the service failed to parse one message because its format was wrong. It was not a one-time error, it kept repeating every few seconds, over and over, with the same message. All the messages that came after it on that partition were not processed at all, so customers were waiting for their order updates. Nobody had deployed anything, and the producer team says only one record looks strange. The business is asking why one bad record can block so many good ones. You are on call. Please walk me through how you would handle it.

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? What did you look at first?
2. What would you monitor afterwards, and what alert would you set?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? Could you lose data or hide problems?

**Key vocabulary**
- consumer lag
- partition / offset
- malformed message
- retry policy / backoff
- blocked partition, "head-of-line blocking"
- dead-letter topic
- deserialization error
- reprocess / replay

**Speaking challenge:** 90 seconds aloud first. Tip: say "offset" as /ˈɔːfset/ and "partition" as /pɑːrˈtɪʃn/ (stress on the second syllable).

## Case M05: "Kafka is exactly-once", but the reconciliation does not match

**Interview prompt.** Hi, this one comes from our finance integration team. A few months ago we built a service that consumes payment events from Kafka and updates our ledger. During design, the team said, "Kafka gives us exactly-once, so we do not need any extra protection against duplicates." Everyone agreed and we moved on. Now the monthly reconciliation with the payment provider shows that our numbers do not match: for some transactions, our records and theirs are different. The finance people are nervous, because the difference is small but it is real money, and auditors will ask about it. There was no big outage that anyone remembers, only a few restarts and one rebalance during a deployment. The team lead says, "But we trusted exactly-once, how is this possible?" You have been asked to lead the investigation. Please walk me through your approach.

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? What data would you compare?
2. What would you monitor afterwards to catch this early next time?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix, and how would you explain the "exactly-once" claim to the team?

**Key vocabulary**
- exactly-once vs at-least-once delivery
- reconciliation / discrepancy
- duplicate processing
- consumer group rebalance
- idempotent consumer
- audit trail
- side effect
- "guarantee" vs "assumption"

**Speaking challenge:** 90 seconds aloud first. Tip: say "idempotent" as /ˌaɪdemˈpoʊtənt/ and "reconciliation" as /ˌrekənsɪliˈeɪʃn/. Practice the sentence: "We trusted a guarantee that did not cover our whole flow."
