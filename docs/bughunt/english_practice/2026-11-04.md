## Case M12: The service freezes under load, but the CPU is idle

**Interview prompt.** Hi, here is an on-call story from our platform team. Around lunchtime our Spring Boot service started to freeze. Requests were not failing fast, they were just hanging, and clients began to time out one after another. The strange part is the load: traffic was high, but the CPU graph was almost flat and low, so the servers looked like they were doing nothing. Memory looked normal and there was no obvious crash or restart. A teammate took a thread dump while the service was frozen, and it was full of threads that were all in a waiting state. Nobody had deployed that day, but traffic had grown over the last few weeks. When the traffic went down in the evening, the service slowly recovered by itself. The manager is asking why a system that is so busy looks so calm. You are on call. Please walk me through how you would handle it.

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? What exactly did you look for in the thread dump?
2. What would you monitor afterwards, and what alert would you set?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? Could it just move the problem somewhere else?

**Key vocabulary**
- thread dump / jstack
- waiting vs blocked vs runnable state
- low CPU under high load
- request timeout / hanging requests
- throughput vs latency
- saturation (of a resource)
- to reproduce under load (load test)
- to recover on its own

**Speaking challenge:** 90 seconds aloud first. Tip: say "thread" as /θred/ (put your tongue between your teeth) and "latency" as /ˈleɪtnsi/. Practice the sentence: "The CPU was low, but the service was not healthy - those are two different things."

## Case P18: Dates parse wrong and the statistics counter is off, only sometimes

**Interview prompt.** Hi, this one comes from our reporting team. A user reported that one of our endpoints "sometimes" returns a wrong date. The same input gives the correct date most of the time, then once in a while the date is parsed incorrectly, or we even get a strange error. On the same service, we also have a statistics counter on a dashboard, and the numbers are slightly lower than the real number of requests. The difference is small but it keeps growing during busy hours. Both problems are random: we cannot reproduce them on a developer laptop with one request at a time, and the unit tests are all green. Logs show nothing suspicious. The product owner says the data looks "a little bit dirty" and wants to know if it is safe to trust the reports. You are the engineer who picked up the ticket. Please walk me through your approach.

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? How did you finally reproduce a problem that only appears sometimes?
2. What would you monitor or test afterwards so this does not come back?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? Is there a cheaper or simpler option, and why did you not choose it?

**Key vocabulary**
- intermittent / non-deterministic bug
- to reproduce (a bug)
- shared state between requests
- thread safety
- race condition
- lost update
- concurrent requests
- regression test

**Speaking challenge:** 90 seconds aloud first. Tip: say "intermittent" as /ˌɪntərˈmɪtənt/ (stress on "mit") and "deterministic" as /dɪˌtɜːrmɪˈnɪstɪk/. Practice the sentence: "It worked on my laptop because I only sent one request at a time."
