## Case P40: Flyway fails on production

**Interview prompt.** Hi, this one is from last Thursday's release. We were deploying a new version of our Spring Boot service to production. The deployment started normally, but the new instances never became healthy and the rollout stopped. When we opened the logs, the application had failed during startup with one clear message: "Migration checksum mismatch". Staging had been fine for weeks, and the developers said their local databases were fine too. Only production refused to start. The release manager was waiting, the business wanted the feature out that day, and one engineer suggested "just wipe the schema history and run it again". Nobody on the team felt comfortable with that. You were called into the incident. Please walk me through how you would handle it.

**Answer frame:** Situation -> What I investigated -> Root cause -> Fix -> How I would prevent it -> What I would tell the team

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? What would you compare, and where would you look?
2. What would you monitor or put in the pipeline afterwards, so you find this before production?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? What is the risk of the quick option the colleague suggested?

**Key vocabulary**
- schema migration / database migration
- checksum mismatch
- to roll back vs. to roll forward
- schema history table
- "the deployment failed on startup"
- to be safe to run in production (idempotent, repeatable)
- change management / peer review of migrations
- blast radius

## Case M09: Lag keeps growing even after we scaled out

**Interview prompt.** Hi, this one is from our payments platform. We have a service that consumes events from a Kafka topic. Over the last few days the consumer lag on that topic has been growing steadily. The dashboards show the line going up, and the downstream teams complain that their data arrives later and later. Yesterday the on-call engineer did the obvious thing: he added more consumer instances. We expected the lag to go down. It did not. The lag kept growing at about the same speed, as if the new instances were not helping at all. CPU on the consumers looks normal and nothing is crashing. The engineering manager asks you, as the most senior person on call, to explain what is going on and what the plan is. Please walk me through how you would approach it.

**Answer frame:** Situation -> What I investigated -> Root cause -> Fix -> How I would prevent it -> What I would tell the team

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? Which metrics, commands or tools would you use?
2. What would you monitor afterwards? Which alert would you set up, and on which number?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? What could get worse, for example ordering or cost?

**Key vocabulary**
- consumer lag
- to scale out vs. to scale up
- throughput vs. latency
- consumer group
- partition assignment / rebalance
- backlog
- "the bottleneck is not where we expected"
- capacity planning
