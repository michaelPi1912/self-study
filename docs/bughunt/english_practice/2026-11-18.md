## Case P11: The transaction history screen gets slower every month

**Interview prompt.** Hi, this one comes from our on-call channel. We have a "Transaction history" screen in our banking-style web app, where customers can see their recent payments. When we launched six months ago, the page loaded in about 50 milliseconds and everyone was happy. Over the last few weeks, support started to receive complaints that the page feels slow. This morning our monitoring showed that the same screen now takes around 4 seconds. Nobody changed the code of this endpoint recently, and there was no new release. The service is up, CPU looks normal, and there are no errors in the logs. The only thing that changed is that the table has been growing every day since launch. The product owner asks you for a quick explanation and a plan. Please walk me through your approach.

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? What evidence would you show the team?
2. What would you monitor afterwards so you catch this kind of slowdown before customers do?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix, and what could it affect on production?

**Key vocabulary**
- query latency / response time degradation
- to analyze the execution plan
- full table scan vs. index scan
- a slow query log
- to roll out a change without downtime
- write overhead / trade-off
- data growth / scalability
- to reproduce with production-like data

## Case P15: The flash sale sold more items than we had in stock

**Interview prompt.** Hi, this one is from last night's flash sale, and the business team is upset. We had a popular product with exactly 10 units left in stock when the sale started. Many customers clicked "Buy" at almost the same moment. After the sale, the finance team counted the orders and found 13 confirmed orders for that product. So we sold 3 items that we do not have. The warehouse cannot ship them, and customer support now has to cancel and apologize. In the application logs there are no exceptions, every request returned success, and the stock check was clearly in the code. It also never happens when a developer tests the flow alone on their laptop. The head of operations asks you, as the backend engineer, to explain what happened and what you will do about it. Please walk me through your approach.

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? Could you reproduce it with a test?
2. What would you monitor or alert on afterwards?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? What happens to throughput when many users buy the same product?

**Key vocabulary**
- flash sale / traffic spike
- to oversell / overselling
- inventory / stock level
- a race condition
- concurrent requests
- to reproduce under load (for example with a multi-threaded test)
- consistency vs. throughput
- idempotent / safe to retry
