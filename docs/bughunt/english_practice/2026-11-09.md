## Case P12: We have an index, but the query still scans the whole table

**Interview prompt.** Hi, this one is from our backend channel. A teammate was looking at a slow login lookup on the `users` table. The query filters with `WHERE LOWER(email) = ?`. There is already an index on the `email` column, so everybody assumed the lookup would be fast. But when he ran `EXPLAIN` on the query, the plan showed a sequential scan over the whole table, not an index scan. The table is growing, and the endpoint is getting slower every week. Nobody changed the index recently, and the query returns correct results. He asked me, "Why is the database ignoring our index?" and I did not have a good answer. You are the engineer who picks this up. Please walk me through how you would handle it.

**Answer frame:** Situation -> What I investigated -> Root cause -> Fix -> How I would prevent it -> What I would tell the team

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? Which tool or command did you use, and what did you look for in the output?
2. What would you monitor afterwards, and how would you check that the fix works on production-size data?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? Does it have a cost for writes, storage or deployment?

**Key vocabulary**
- execution plan / query plan
- sequential scan vs. index scan
- to run `EXPLAIN` (`EXPLAIN ANALYZE`)
- selectivity / cardinality
- query optimizer (planner)
- slow query log
- to roll out a change safely (on a large table)
- regression

**Pronunciation tips:** "cardinality" = /ˌkɑːrdɪˈnælɪti/; "sequential" = /sɪˈkwenʃəl/; "analyze" = /ˈænəlaɪz/ (stress on the first syllable).

## Case P29: The audit says we must change how passwords are stored

**Interview prompt.** Hi, this is a request that landed on our team this week. Our security auditors finished their review of the user service. One of their findings is about how we store user passwords. They say the current approach is not acceptable anymore, and they want us to change it. There is one hard constraint from the business side: we are not allowed to force users to reset their passwords. People log in every day, and a mass reset would flood support and hurt trust. Users must keep logging in with the same password they have today, and nobody should notice anything. There is a deadline from the auditors, but the system is live and serves real customers. You are the engineer who owns this. Please walk me through how you would plan and deliver it.

**Answer frame:** Situation -> What I investigated -> Root cause -> Fix -> How I would prevent it -> What I would tell the team

**Follow-up questions**
1. How would you prove, before and after the change, that existing users can still log in and that nothing is broken?
2. What would you monitor during and after the rollout, and what is your rollback plan?
3. What is the trade-off of your approach? What risks remain, and what would you tell the auditors and the team?

**Key vocabulary**
- audit finding / compliance requirement
- to migrate without downtime / zero-downtime migration
- backward compatible
- gradual rollout / phased rollout
- rollback plan
- security posture
- to authenticate / authentication flow
- technical debt

**Pronunciation tips:** "authenticate" = /ɔːˈθentɪkeɪt/ (the "th" is /θ/); "compliance" = /kəmˈplaɪəns/; "migration" = /maɪˈɡreɪʃən/.
