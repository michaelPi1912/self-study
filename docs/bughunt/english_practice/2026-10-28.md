## Case P36: The consumer got the event, but the order was not there

**Interview prompt.** Hi, let's go through an incident from last week. Our order service publishes a "new order" event to Kafka whenever a customer places an order. A downstream service consumes that event and then loads the order from the database to continue processing, for example to prepare shipping. Some time ago the support team started reporting odd cases: the consumer clearly received the "new order" event, but when it queried the database, the order simply was not there. When the team retried a few minutes later, the order existed. The problem is not constant; it shows up randomly, more often during busy hours. Nothing looks wrong in the producer's logs, and nobody sees errors in Kafka itself. You are the engineer on call. Please walk me through how you would investigate, and what you would tell the team.

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? What would you look at in the logs or in a test?
2. What would you monitor afterwards to notice a similar problem early?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? Does it fully remove the risk, or is something left?

**Key vocabulary**
- event-driven architecture / message broker
- to publish / to consume an event
- race condition
- transaction boundary
- eventual consistency
- at-least-once delivery
- idempotent consumer
- to reproduce intermittently (intermittent failure)

## Case M18: The morning report makes the whole system slow

**Interview prompt.** Hi, here is a scenario from our production system. Every morning, a scheduled job generates business reports for the management team. Since a few weeks ago, the on-call channel lights up at the same time every day: during the report run, our order API becomes about five times slower. Customers feel it at checkout, and a few requests even time out. After the report finishes, everything goes back to normal, until the next morning. Nobody deployed anything new on those days, and the traffic on the order API looks about the same as usual. The product owner says the reports are important and cannot be dropped. As a middle-level engineer, how would you investigate this, and what would you propose to the team?

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? What data would you collect, and from where?
2. What would you monitor afterwards, and which alert would you add?
3. What is the trade-off of your solution? What new risk or cost does it introduce?

**Key vocabulary**
- peak load / resource contention
- latency spike (p95 / p99 latency)
- slow query / execution plan
- workload isolation
- to offload (a workload)
- connection pool
- scheduled job / batch window
- capacity planning
