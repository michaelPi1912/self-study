## Case P38: Large Excel upload takes the server down

**Interview prompt.** Hi, imagine you are the on-call engineer this week. It is Monday morning and our finance team is panicking. Late last night, one of the users uploaded an Excel file of about 50 MB through our import page, and a few minutes later the whole server went down. The service restarted by itself, but the same thing happened again when they retried the upload. Smaller files, a few megabytes each, have always worked fine. Other users who were using the app at that time got errors, too, so this is not only one person's problem. The customer needs their import done today, and the management wants to know what happened. You are the first person to look at this. Please walk me through how you would handle it.

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove that this was the real cause, and not just a correlation with the big file?
2. What would you monitor after the fix, and which alert would you set up first?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? Does it change anything for the user?

**Key vocabulary**
- out of memory (OOM) / heap exhaustion
- heap dump / thread dump
- memory footprint
- to reproduce the issue locally
- root cause vs. symptom
- to roll out a fix / to roll back
- upper bound / hard limit
- post-mortem (incident review)

## Case P30: Partner service hangs and our threads run out

**Interview prompt.** Hi, let's do an incident scenario. Our service calls an external partner's API during a normal user request. This afternoon the partner had problems and their API stopped answering. It did not return errors, it just hung. Within minutes, our service became slower and slower. The number of free threads in our pool kept going down until almost none were left. Then the health check started to fail, the load balancer marked our instances as unhealthy, and users saw errors on pages that do not even use the partner. When the partner recovered, we needed a restart before things went back to normal. You are on call. Please tell me how you would investigate, explain what happened to your team lead, and what you would change.

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove that the partner call was the cause, and what did you look at first (metrics, logs, thread dump)?
2. What would you monitor afterwards so you notice this earlier next time?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? What can go wrong for the user when your protection kicks in?

**Key vocabulary**
- thread pool exhaustion
- thread dump / blocked threads
- downstream dependency / upstream
- cascading failure
- graceful degradation
- health check / liveness vs. readiness probe
- fail fast
- resilience patterns (you may name the ones you know)
