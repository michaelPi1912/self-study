## Case P23: Two users see each other's search results

**Interview prompt.** Hi, this is a serious one from our support team. This morning two customers contacted us separately. Each of them said the search page showed results that clearly belonged to someone else. One user saw another person's order references, and the other user saw results they never searched for. At first support thought it was a user mistake, but then a second pair of users reported the same thing, so we know it is real. It does not happen on every search, and it is hard to trigger on purpose. The database itself looks correct: when we query it directly, each user has the right data. Nothing was deployed today, and there are no errors in the logs. Security has been pulled into the call because this might be a data exposure. You are the engineer on call. Please walk me through how you would handle it.

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? What exactly did you do to reproduce it safely?
2. What would you monitor or test afterwards so this does not come back?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? And what would you tell security and the affected users?

**Key vocabulary**
- data leak / data exposure
- to reproduce (a bug) in a safe environment
- cache hit vs cache miss
- stale data
- to isolate the blast radius
- incident report / post-mortem
- to escalate to the security team
- regression test

**Speaking challenge:** 90 seconds aloud first. Tip: say "cache" as /kæʃ/ (like "cash"), not "ca-che". Practice the sentence: "The database was correct, so the problem had to be somewhere between the database and the user."

## Case P31: The partner service recovered, then crashed again

**Interview prompt.** Hi, here is a story from our integrations team. We depend on an external partner service for payments. Yesterday the partner had an outage for about twenty minutes, and our service started to log failures, which is expected. Then the partner's engineers fixed the problem and their service came back online. But within a minute it went down again. They brought it back a second time, and again it collapsed almost immediately. Their team told us that, right at the moment of recovery, they saw thousands of requests from our side arriving at the same time. Our own dashboard showed a sharp spike in outgoing calls exactly when the partner became healthy. The partner is now angry, and our account manager wants an explanation and a plan. You are the engineer who owns the integration. Please walk me through your approach.

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? What data or graph would you show the partner?
2. What would you monitor afterwards, and what alert would you set?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? Could it make our own users wait longer?

**Key vocabulary**
- outage / downtime
- a spike in outgoing traffic
- to hammer a service (informal)
- thundering herd
- cascading failure
- graceful degradation
- to apologise to a partner / to set expectations
- post-mortem

**Speaking challenge:** 90 seconds aloud first. Tip: say "thundering" as /ˈθʌndərɪŋ/ and "cascading" as /kæsˈkeɪdɪŋ/. Practice the sentence: "The partner was healthy again, but our traffic made it sick a second time."
