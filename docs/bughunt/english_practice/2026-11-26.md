## Case P28: I changed one number in the URL and saw someone else's order

**Interview prompt.** Hi, this one comes from our security champion, and it is a little uncomfortable. A customer-support colleague was testing our web shop with a normal customer account. She opened her own order at /orders/101, and everything looked fine. Out of curiosity she changed the URL to /orders/102, and the page loaded an order that belongs to a completely different customer, with a name, an address and the items. She did not need any special tool, just the browser address bar. She is already logged in with her own account, and the login itself works as designed. The data is personal, so this may become a privacy incident. The engineering manager wants a clear explanation, a fix, and a plan so we do not repeat it. Please walk me through your approach.

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? How would you reproduce it safely?
2. What would you monitor afterwards to detect someone trying this on a larger scale?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? Does it add cost or complexity, and where else might the same weakness exist?

**Key vocabulary**
- authentication vs. authorization
- access control / ownership check
- to enumerate IDs (sequential identifiers)
- privacy incident / data exposure
- principle of least privilege
- to reproduce (an issue) with two test users
- regression test
- to audit (similar endpoints)

**Speaking challenge:** read your answer aloud for 90 seconds. Practise "authentication" (/ɔːˌθentɪˈkeɪʃn/), "authorization" (/ˌɔːθərəˈzeɪʃn/) and "enumerate" (/ɪˈnjuːməreɪt/).

## Case J07: Orders placed at 11 p.m. appear on the next day

**Interview prompt.** Hi, this one comes from the finance team, and they sound confused. Our customers are in Vietnam. A customer placed an order at 11 p.m. local time on the 14th. In the daily report, the same order is listed under the 15th. This does not happen for every order, mostly the ones placed late in the evening. The numbers in the report are otherwise correct, so finance cannot tell if the totals for each day can be trusted. They already tried refreshing the report and exporting it again, and the date is still wrong. The month-end closing is in three days, so the pressure is real. The team lead asks you to find out what is happening and propose a fix. Please walk me through your approach.

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? What would your test look like?
2. What would you monitor or check afterwards, and what about the orders already stored?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? Does it change how we store or display data?

**Key vocabulary**
- time zone / UTC offset
- to store timestamps in UTC
- to convert (a time) between zones
- off-by-one-day error
- reporting boundary (start and end of the day)
- to reproduce with a fixed clock
- data migration / backfill
- edge case (late evening, daylight saving time)

**Speaking challenge:** read your answer aloud for 90 seconds. Practise "timezone" (/ˈtaɪmzoʊn/), "offset" (/ˈɒfset/) and "boundary" (/ˈbaʊndri/).
