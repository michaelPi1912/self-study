## Case P34: Some orders end up in the wrong final status

**Interview prompt.** Hi, this one came from the customer-support channel. Our order system is event-driven: services publish events to Kafka, and an order-status service consumes them to keep the order status up to date. This week support noticed a handful of strange orders. A customer created an order and cancelled it almost immediately. The customer's app shows the cancellation, but in our back-office the order ends with the wrong final status. When we looked at the events, we saw that the order-status service received the "CANCELLED" event before the "CREATED" event for the same order. It does not happen all the time, only for some orders, mostly when users act quickly. There are no exceptions in the logs and Kafka looks healthy. The warehouse team is worried because a cancelled order might still get picked and shipped. Please walk me through your approach.

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? Could you reproduce it locally?
2. What would you monitor afterwards to detect it early?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? What do you give up in throughput or complexity?

**Key vocabulary**
- event ordering / out-of-order events
- a partition key
- to consume / to publish an event
- a race condition between two events
- idempotent consumer
- a state machine / a valid state transition
- a consumer lag
- to reproduce the issue locally

## Case P08: One slow partner brings the whole system to a halt

**Interview prompt.** Hi, this is an incident from last Thursday. We integrate with an external partner through one single endpoint in our application. That afternoon the partner's service became slow, around 20 seconds per response. We expected only that one endpoint to be affected. Instead the whole application started to hang: unrelated pages and APIs timed out, health checks became slow, and users could not log in. Our own CPU stayed low and there were no crashes. After the partner recovered, everything went back to normal on its own. Management asks two questions: why did a problem in one small feature take down everything, and how do we make sure it does not happen again with the next slow partner? Please walk me through your approach.

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? What would you look at during the incident (metrics, thread dump, logs)?
2. What would you monitor and alert on afterwards?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? What happens to the user of the slow endpoint?

**Key vocabulary**
- a cascading failure
- to exhaust a resource pool
- a thread dump
- a timeout / a circuit breaker
- to isolate a failure (bulkhead)
- latency vs. throughput
- a blast radius
- graceful degradation
