## Case J01: The cache says "not found" for a user who is online

**Interview prompt.** Hi, this one is from our on-call channel. We keep a small in-memory cache of active users so we do not hit the database on every request. This morning, support reported a strange case: a customer was clearly online and using the app, but one of our internal tools said "user not found" for them. The support engineer checked and saw that the customer had changed their email address a minute earlier. Before that change everything worked, and after it the lookup in the cache failed every time. Other users, who did not change anything, were found without problems. Nothing crashed, there are no exceptions in the logs, and the database record is correct. The team lead asks you to take a look and explain it to the support team in simple words. Please walk me through your approach.

**Answer frame:** Situation -> What I investigated -> Root cause -> Fix -> How I would prevent it -> What I would tell the team

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? Could you reproduce it with a small test?
2. What would you monitor or test afterwards so a similar problem does not come back?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? What would you change in the design, and what could it affect?

**Key vocabulary**
- in-memory cache / lookup
- to reproduce the issue with a unit test
- mutable vs. immutable object
- `equals()` and `hashCode()` contract
- to silently fail (no exception, wrong result)
- a hash-based collection
- "to narrow down the problem"
- "a subtle bug"

## Case P27: Users can read data, but every write returns 403

**Interview prompt.** Hi, this is a story from our API team. After a release, the mobile team told us that something is wrong with the backend. Users can log in and they can browse everything: all the GET endpoints work. But every time they try to create or update something, so any POST or PUT request, the server answers with 403 Forbidden. It happens for normal users who are fully logged in with a valid session or token, not only for a few accounts. The login itself works, and there are no errors in the application logs that look like a crash. Support is receiving many tickets, and the product manager asks whether we should roll back the release. You are the on-call engineer. Please walk me through what you would do and what you would tell the team.

**Answer frame:** Situation -> What I investigated -> Root cause -> Fix -> How I would prevent it -> What I would tell the team

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? What would you compare between a working request and a failing one?
2. What would you monitor afterwards? Which signal tells you early that this is happening again?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? How do you make sure you do not weaken security while fixing it?

**Key vocabulary**
- 401 Unauthorized vs. 403 Forbidden
- authentication vs. authorization
- to reproduce with `curl` or Postman
- request headers / request payload
- to compare a working and a failing request
- to roll back a release vs. to roll forward
- security filter chain
- "to rule out" a possible cause
