## Case M08: One consumer is drowning, the others are idle

**Interview prompt.** Hi, this one comes from the platform team, and it is a bit stressful. We run an event pipeline on Kafka, and a consumer group processes the messages. This morning the monitoring dashboard showed something odd: one partition has a lag of several million messages and it keeps growing. At the same time, all the other partitions are almost empty, and their consumers are sitting idle. We already have enough consumers, and CPU on the cluster looks fine overall. Adding more consumer instances did not help at all, which surprised everybody. Downstream teams are now complaining that their data arrives hours late. The engineering manager asks you to explain what is happening and how we get the lag down without breaking anything. Please walk me through your approach.

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? What data or metrics did you look at?
2. What would you monitor afterwards to be sure the problem does not come back?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? What do we gain, and what do we risk giving up?

**Key vocabulary**
- consumer lag
- partition / consumer group
- uneven load (an unbalanced workload)
- throughput vs. latency
- to scale out (horizontally)
- message ordering guarantee
- to drain a backlog
- trade-off (to trade X for Y)

**Speaking challenge:** read your answer aloud for 90 seconds. Practise "partition" (/pɑːrˈtɪʃn/), "latency" (/ˈleɪtnsi/) and "throughput" (/ˈθruːpʊt/).

## Case P49: An error in service C, and nobody knows where it started

**Interview prompt.** Hi, this one is from the on-call channel at 2 a.m. Our system has several services: a request comes in at the first service, it calls a second one over HTTP, and then a third one, service C, handles part of the work after an event is published to Kafka. Tonight service C logged a serious error for one order. The customer wrote to support, and support wants to know what the customer actually did. We have logs in all three services, but when we open the log of service C we cannot tell which original request caused the error. There are thousands of similar lines every minute, and the timestamps are only close, not exact. Two engineers have been scrolling through logs for an hour. The team lead asks you how you would solve this problem tonight, and what you would change so it never takes an hour again. Please walk me through your approach.

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove your approach works? How would you test it across all three services?
2. What would you monitor afterwards? What would "good" look like on a dashboard?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? Is there any cost in performance, effort, or log volume?

**Key vocabulary**
- on-call / incident
- to trace a request end to end
- log aggregation / searchable logs
- observability (logs, metrics, traces)
- hop (a call between two services)
- to propagate (information) across boundaries
- to reproduce locally
- mean time to resolution (MTTR)
