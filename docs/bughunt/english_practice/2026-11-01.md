## Case P33: Some orders got their bonus points twice

**Interview prompt.** Hi, here is a real incident from our team. We run an order platform where services talk to each other through Kafka. Our loyalty service consumes order events and adds bonus points to the customer's account. This morning, customer support forwarded a ticket: several customers say their bonus points were added two times for a single order. Finance confirmed it from the data: the duplicates are not random across the day. They all appear after a rebalance of the consumer group, which happened during last night's deployment. Most orders are fine, only some orders were rewarded twice. The business is worried because the points have real monetary value. You are the engineer on call, and the team lead asks you to explain what you think happened and what you will do. Please walk me through it.

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? What would you look at in the logs, offsets, or data?
2. What would you monitor afterwards so we find out quickly if it happens again?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? What does it cost in complexity or performance?

**Key vocabulary**
- consumer group rebalance
- offset commit
- at-least-once delivery
- duplicate message / duplicate processing
- to deduplicate
- side effect
- to reconcile the data (and correct the affected customers)
- consumer lag

## Case M25: An error that happens only on production

**Interview prompt.** Hi, this one is a classic. Our API serves a few million requests per day. The monitoring dashboard shows a small but steady number of errors: about 0.1% of requests fail. Customers complain once in a while, and the support team has a few screenshots. The tricky part is that nobody on the team can reproduce it. You run the same endpoint locally with the same kind of data, and it works every time. The staging environment looks healthy too. The failures are not tied to one customer that we know of, and the logs we have today do not clearly explain why a request fails. The manager says, "It is only 0.1%, but it is real and it is hurting trust." You have been asked to take ownership of this problem. How do you approach it, and what do you tell the team?

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? What evidence would convince a skeptical teammate?
2. What would you monitor afterwards, and what would you change in our logging or tracing?
3. What is the trade-off of your approach? Think about cost, noise, and performance overhead.

**Key vocabulary**
- observability (logs, metrics, traces)
- correlation ID / trace ID
- structured logging
- distributed tracing
- error rate / percentile latency (p99)
- to reproduce an issue (intermittent / flaky)
- to narrow down the scope
- production-like environment
