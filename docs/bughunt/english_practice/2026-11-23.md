## Case P16: One admin's product edit disappears

**Interview prompt.** Hi, this one came from the content team this afternoon. Two admins were editing the same product in the back office at almost the same time. The first admin changed the description and the stock note and clicked Save. She saw the "Saved successfully" message, and no error appeared on screen. A few minutes later she opened the product again and her changes were gone. Only the second admin's edit was there. We checked the logs and there are no exceptions and no failed requests, and both saves returned HTTP 200. The two admins are now arguing about who changed what, and the team is worried that the same thing is happening on other products without anyone noticing. The content lead asks you to explain what happened and how we make sure nobody silently loses work again. Please walk me through your approach.

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? Could you reproduce it on your machine?
2. What would you monitor afterwards to find out if it is still happening?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? What does the second admin experience now?

**Key vocabulary**
- silent data loss
- two users editing at the same time
- to reproduce a race condition
- the first/last write wins
- HTTP 200 but wrong data
- audit log / change history
- to reproduce with two browser sessions
- user experience vs. data safety

**Speaking challenge:** read your answer aloud for 90 seconds. Practise "concurrent" (/kənˈkɜːrənt/), "simultaneously" (/ˌsaɪməlˈteɪniəsli/) and "race condition" (/reɪs kənˈdɪʃn/).

## Case J06: The cleanup job throws an exception

**Interview prompt.** Hi, this one is from the operations team. We have a scheduled job that cleans up expired orders. It loops over a list of orders with a for-each loop, and when it finds an expired order, it calls `list.remove()` on that list. Since last night the job crashes with a `ConcurrentModificationException`. The strange part is that it does not crash every time: on some nights it works, and on other nights it fails halfway through. When it fails, some expired orders are cleaned and some are still there, so the data is in a half-finished state. Operations see the alert in the morning and ask you for two things: an explanation of what happened, and a fix before tonight's run. Please walk me through your approach.

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? Why does it fail on some nights and not on others?
2. How would you test the fix? What edge cases would you try, for example an empty list or the last element?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? Is there a difference in readability or performance?

**Key vocabulary**
- ConcurrentModificationException
- to iterate over a collection
- for-each loop / iterator
- scheduled job / batch job
- intermittent failure
- partial update / half-finished state
- edge case (empty list, last element)
- unit test / regression test

**Speaking challenge:** say your six steps aloud in 90 seconds. Practise "iterate" (/ˈɪtəreɪt/), "iterator" (/ˈɪtəreɪtər/) and "intermittent" (/ˌɪntərˈmɪtənt/).
