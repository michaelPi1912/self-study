## Case J20: The order endpoint crashes with StackOverflowError

**Interview prompt.** Hi, this one came from the mobile team this morning. They call our endpoint that returns an order with its items, and instead of JSON they get an HTTP 500. In our logs there is a `StackOverflowError`, and the stack trace is huge: it repeats the same few frames again and again, going from Order to Item, back to Order, then Item again, and so on until the thread runs out of stack. Our unit tests for the service layer are green, and loading the order from the database works fine. The problem only appears when the controller returns the object to the client. The mobile team is blocked on their release. They ask you to explain what is happening and when they can expect a fix. Please walk me through your approach.

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? What did you look at in the stack trace?
2. How would you make sure this kind of problem is caught before production?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? Does it change the API contract for the mobile team?

**Key vocabulary**
- stack trace / repeating frames
- StackOverflowError
- to serialize / serialization
- HTTP 500 / server-side error
- to reproduce locally
- API contract / response shape
- regression test
- to unblock the team

## Case P22: The product page shows the old price for 10 minutes

**Interview prompt.** Hi, this one is from the pricing team. A product manager updated the price of a popular product in the admin panel. The admin panel shows the new price and the save was successful. But the public product page kept showing the old price, and it stayed that way for about 10 minutes. After that, the page showed the new price by itself, without anyone doing anything. Customers saw different prices on the page and at checkout during that time, and one of them posted a screenshot on social media. There are no errors in the logs. The pricing lead asks you to explain what happened and make sure it does not happen again at the next big sale. Please walk me through your approach.

**Follow-up questions**
1. How did you prove it was the cause? Could you reproduce the 10-minute delay?
2. What would you monitor afterwards to detect stale data early?
3. What is the trade-off of your fix? What do you give up in performance or complexity?

**Key vocabulary**
- stale data / stale price
- cache TTL (time to live)
- cache invalidation / eviction
- consistency vs. performance
- source of truth
- to reproduce the delay
- cache hit ratio
- eventual consistency

**Speaking challenge:** read your answer aloud for 90 seconds. Practise "cache" (/kæʃ/, like "cash"), "TTL" (say each letter: T-T-L), and "invalidate" (/ɪnˈvælɪdeɪt/).
